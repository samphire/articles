=== medium-medium-01 | The Benefits of Walking
Walking is one of the simplest forms of exercise, but it has a surprising number of benefits. Unlike many sports, it doesn't require any special equipment, and almost anyone can do it.

Doctors say that a brisk walk of thirty minutes a day can lower the risk of heart disease and help to control weight. Walking also has a positive effect on mental health. Many people find that a walk in a park or along a river helps them to relax and think more clearly. Some writers and scientists have even said that their best ideas came to them while they were walking.

Another advantage is that walking can easily become part of a daily routine. Instead of taking the bus for a short journey, you can walk. Instead of using the lift, you can take the stairs. These small changes add up over time.

Of course, walking is not the answer to every health problem. However, for people who are looking for an easy way to become more active, it is an excellent place to start.

=== medium-medium-02 | Working from Home
In the past few years, the number of people who work from home has grown enormously. For some, it has been a positive change, while others have found it difficult.

One obvious benefit is that home workers don't have to commute. A person who used to spend an hour on a crowded train each morning now has extra time for sleep, exercise, or family. Many people also say that they can concentrate better without the noise of an office.

On the other hand, working from home can be lonely. Without colleagues around, it is easy to feel isolated. Some people also find it hard to separate work from the rest of their life. When the office is also the living room, it can be tempting to check emails late at night.

For this reason, many companies now offer a mixed system. Employees work in the office for part of the week and at home for the rest. This seems to be a sensible compromise, as it gives workers both flexibility and the chance to see their team in person.

=== medium-medium-03 | The Story of the Sandwich
According to a popular story, the sandwich is named after an English nobleman called the Earl of Sandwich, who lived in the eighteenth century. It is said that he loved playing cards so much that he didn't want to stop for a meal. Instead, he asked a servant to bring him a piece of meat between two slices of bread. That way, he could eat with one hand and continue playing with the other.

Historians are not sure whether this story is completely true. Some believe that the Earl actually ate this way while he was working at his desk. Either way, the name became popular, and soon people all over the country were asking for "the same as Sandwich."

Of course, the idea of putting food between pieces of bread is much older than the Earl. People in many cultures had eaten similar meals for centuries. However, it was in the eighteenth century that the sandwich got its name and became a fashionable snack.

Today, the sandwich is one of the most common lunches in the world, with an almost endless number of variations.

=== medium-medium-05 | Why Do Leaves Change Colour?
Every autumn, the leaves on many trees turn from green to brilliant shades of red, orange, and yellow. It is one of the most beautiful sights of the year, but what causes it?

During the spring and summer, leaves contain a large amount of a green substance called chlorophyll. Chlorophyll allows the tree to use sunlight to make food. It is so strong that it hides the other colours in the leaf.

As the days become shorter and the temperature drops, the tree stops producing chlorophyll. The green colour slowly disappears, and the yellow and orange colours that were there all along become visible. Some trees also produce a new red pigment in the autumn, which is why maple leaves can look so bright.

Eventually, the tree cuts off the supply of water to the leaves, and they fall to the ground. This helps the tree to save energy and water during the cold winter months. In the spring, the whole process begins again with a new set of leaves.

=== medium-medium-08 | Kimchi and the Kimchi Fridge
Kimchi is the most famous dish in Korean cuisine, and many families still make their own. Traditionally, kimchi was stored in large clay pots called onggi, which were buried in the ground during the winter. The soil kept the temperature low and stable, allowing the vegetables to ferment slowly.

As more people moved into apartments in the cities, burying a pot in the garden was no longer possible. An ordinary fridge was not a perfect solution either, because its temperature changed every time someone opened the door.

In the 1990s, Korean companies began to sell a special appliance called a kimchi refrigerator. It is designed to copy the conditions of an onggi under the ground. It keeps the temperature very stable and lets the owner control how quickly the kimchi ferments.

Today, a large number of Korean households have a kimchi fridge in addition to a normal one. Many people use it to store fruit, vegetables, and even rice as well. It is a good example of how a modern invention can help to keep an old tradition alive.

=== medium-long-01 | The Four-Day Working Week
In recent years, a number of companies around the world have experimented with a four-day working week. Employees work for four days instead of five, but they receive the same salary. To many people, this sounds too good to be true. However, the results of several trials have been surprisingly positive.

Supporters of the idea argue that a shorter week makes workers happier and healthier. With an extra day off, people have more time to rest, exercise, and spend time with their families. As a result, they are less likely to suffer from stress and burnout. In some trials, the number of sick days fell significantly.

There is also evidence that productivity does not necessarily decrease. When employees know they have only four days to finish their work, they tend to focus better and waste less time. Many companies in the trials cut down on long meetings and unnecessary emails. Some managers reported that their teams produced the same amount of work, or even more, in less time.

However, the four-day week is not suitable for every kind of business. A hospital, for example, must be open every day, and a shop cannot simply close on Fridays. In these cases, a shorter week would mean hiring more staff, which increases costs. Critics also worry that some employees might try to fit five days of work into four, which could actually increase stress rather than reduce it.

Another challenge is fairness. If office workers enjoy a three-day weekend while factory workers do not, it could create tension within a company.

Despite these concerns, interest in the idea continues to grow. Several governments have funded trials, and some companies have made the change permanent after the trial ended. It is too early to say whether the four-day week will become the norm, but it has certainly started an important conversation about the way we work.

=== medium-long-02 | The Man Who Planted Trees
On a river island in the north-east of India, there is a forest that did not exist forty years ago. It was planted, tree by tree, by one man. As a teenager, he saw a group of snakes that had died from the heat on a sandbank in the river, because there were no trees to give them shade. The sight shocked him, and he decided to do something about it.

He began by planting bamboo on the empty sandbank. Every day, he carried water from the river to the young plants. Many of them died, but he kept going. Over the years, he planted thousands of trees, including many different species. Slowly, the bare land turned into a forest.

As the forest grew, animals began to return. Birds built their nests in the trees, and deer and rabbits came to live there. Later, even a herd of elephants started to visit the forest every year. Tigers and rhinos have also been seen there.

For a long time, almost nobody knew about the forest. The man lived a simple life with his family, keeping cows and selling milk. It was only when a wildlife photographer came across the forest by chance that the story became known. Journalists from around the world came to interview him, and he received a national award for his work.

His story shows the remarkable difference that one person can make. He had no money, no machines, and no help from the government. He only had patience and a strong belief that the land could be saved.

Today, he still visits the forest every day and continues to plant new trees. When people ask him why he does it, his answer is simple. He says that trees give us life, and that if we protect nature, nature will protect us.

=== medium-long-03 | How Sleep Affects Learning
Many students believe that the best way to prepare for an exam is to stay up all night studying. However, scientists who study the brain say that this is one of the worst things a student can do. Sleep, it turns out, plays a vital role in learning and memory.

During the day, the brain takes in a huge amount of new information. Much of this information is stored temporarily in an area called the hippocampus. While we sleep, the brain replays the events of the day and moves important information to other parts of the brain, where it can be stored for the long term. Without enough sleep, this process does not happen properly, and much of what we learned may be lost.

In one well-known type of experiment, two groups of people learn a list of words or a new skill. One group then sleeps for a full night, while the other stays awake. When both groups are tested the next day, the group that slept usually remembers significantly more.

Sleep also affects our ability to concentrate. A tired student finds it harder to pay attention in class and makes more mistakes. Even a single night of poor sleep can reduce performance on a test.

So how much sleep do young people need? Most experts recommend that teenagers get between eight and ten hours a night. Unfortunately, many teenagers get far less, partly because of homework, after-school activities, and the use of phones late at night. The light from a screen can also make it harder to fall asleep.

The message from scientists is clear. If you want to do well in an exam, it is better to study in short sessions over several weeks and get a good night's sleep before the test. An all-night study session might feel productive, but it is likely to do more harm than good.

=== medium-long-04 | Hangeul: An Alphabet with a Birthday
Most writing systems developed slowly over hundreds or even thousands of years. Hangeul, the Korean alphabet, is unusual because it was created on purpose, at a specific time, by a specific person. It was invented in the fifteenth century under the direction of King Sejong the Great, and it was announced to the public in 1446.

Before Hangeul, Koreans wrote using Chinese characters. The problem was that Chinese and Korean are very different languages, and learning thousands of characters took many years of study. As a result, only a small number of people, mostly from the upper class, could read and write. King Sejong wanted to create a writing system that ordinary people could learn quickly.

The alphabet that he and his scholars designed is remarkably logical. The shapes of the consonants are based on the position of the mouth and tongue when the sounds are made. For example, the letter for the "g" sound shows the back of the tongue touching the roof of the mouth. The vowels are built from three basic symbols representing the sky, the earth, and a human being.

Not everyone welcomed the new alphabet. Some scholars argued that using it would damage the relationship with China and lower the status of educated people. For several centuries, Chinese characters remained the official way of writing, and Hangeul was often seen as a script for women and children.

However, in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries, Hangeul gradually became the main writing system of Korea. Today, almost every Korean adult can read and write. Every year on the ninth of October, South Koreans celebrate Hangeul Day, a national holiday dedicated to the alphabet. It may be one of the only writing systems in the world with its own birthday.

=== medium-long-05 | The Problem with Fast Fashion
Every year, the fashion industry produces billions of items of clothing. Much of it is what experts call "fast fashion": cheap, trendy clothes that are designed to be worn only a few times before being thrown away. While fast fashion has made new clothes affordable for many people, it has also created serious problems.

The first problem is waste. Because the clothes are so cheap, many people buy more than they need. A large proportion of these items end up in landfill within a year or two. Many fabrics, such as polyester, are made from plastic and can take hundreds of years to break down.

The second problem is the effect on the environment during production. Growing cotton requires an enormous amount of water. It is often said that producing a single cotton T-shirt can use thousands of litres. In addition, the chemicals used to dye fabrics are sometimes released into rivers, polluting the water that local people depend on.

There are also concerns about working conditions. To keep prices low, many garments are made in factories where workers earn very little and work long hours. In some cases, the factories have been unsafe, leading to tragic accidents.

So what can consumers do? One solution is simply to buy less and to choose clothes that will last for a long time. Another is to buy second-hand clothes from a charity shop or an online market. Repairing a torn shirt or swapping clothes with friends can also make a difference.

Some fashion brands have started to respond to these concerns by using recycled materials and offering repair services. However, critics say that real change will only happen when people's attitude towards clothing changes. As one campaigner put it, the most sustainable item of clothing is the one that is already in your wardrobe.

=== medium-long-07 | The Honeybee Crisis
Honeybees are small, but they play an enormous role in our lives. As they fly from flower to flower collecting nectar, they carry pollen from one plant to another. This process, known as pollination, allows many plants to produce fruit and seeds. It is estimated that around a third of the food we eat depends, at least in part, on pollinators such as bees.

In recent decades, however, beekeepers in many countries have reported large losses of bees. In some years, a significant proportion of colonies have died over the winter. Scientists have been trying to understand why.

There seems to be no single cause. One major factor is the use of certain pesticides, which can harm the bees' ability to find their way back to the hive. Another is a tiny parasite called the varroa mite, which attacks bees and spreads disease. Habitat loss is also important. As fields of wildflowers are replaced by roads, buildings, and huge farms growing a single crop, bees find it harder to get the variety of food they need.

Climate change may make the problem worse. Warmer temperatures can cause flowers to bloom earlier in the year, before bees are active, or allow new diseases to spread.

The good news is that there are many ways to help. Some countries have restricted the use of the pesticides that are most harmful to bees. Farmers are being encouraged to leave strips of wildflowers around their fields. In cities, people are setting up hives on rooftops and planting flowers on their balconies.

Even individuals can make a difference. Planting a variety of flowers, avoiding chemical sprays in the garden, and buying honey from local beekeepers are all simple steps. Protecting bees is not only about saving an insect; it is about protecting the food supply for future generations.

=== medium-long-08 | The Orchestra of Recycled Instruments
In a small town in Paraguay, a community grew up beside a huge landfill site. Many families earned a living by collecting and selling rubbish. There was little money for anything else, and certainly not for musical instruments. A violin, for example, could cost more than a family's house.

A local music teacher had an idea. Together with a rubbish collector who was skilled at making things, he began to build instruments out of the waste from the landfill. They made violins from old oil cans and pieces of wood. A cello was made from a metal barrel, and the keys of a saxophone were made from bottle caps and coins. The instruments looked unusual, but the sound they produced was surprisingly good.

The teacher started to give lessons to the children of the community. For many of them, it was the first time they had ever had the chance to learn music. Practice gave them a reason to stay in school and a safe place to spend their afternoons.

Over the years, the group became an orchestra. A documentary film about them was made, and the orchestra began to receive invitations from abroad. The children, some of whom had never left their town, performed on stages in many countries, playing classical music as well as popular songs.

The orchestra has become a symbol of what creativity can achieve. It shows that talent exists everywhere, even in the poorest places, and that what many people see as rubbish can be turned into something beautiful.

The teacher often says that the world sends them its garbage, and they send back music. His words are a reminder that the value of something often depends on how we choose to look at it.

=== medium-long-09 | Should Homework Be Banned?
Homework has been a part of school life for generations, but in recent years, some teachers and parents have begun to question whether it is really necessary. A few schools have even decided to stop giving homework altogether, especially to younger children. The debate is a lively one, with strong arguments on both sides.

Supporters of homework say that it helps students to practise what they have learned in class. A maths lesson, for example, may introduce a new skill, but students need to use it many times before they can do it confidently. Homework also teaches students to manage their time and to work independently, skills that will be important at university and in their future careers. In addition, it gives parents a chance to see what their children are studying.

Critics, however, argue that too much homework can do more harm than good. Many students already spend a long day at school, followed by extra classes in the evening. When they finally get home, they are tired, and a large pile of homework can cause stress and take away time for sleep, exercise, and family. Some research suggests that for young children in particular, homework has little effect on how well they do at school.

There is also the question of fairness. Some students have a quiet room, a computer, and parents who can help them. Others have none of these things. Homework may therefore increase the gap between students from different backgrounds.

Perhaps the answer lies somewhere in the middle. Many educators now believe that the quality of homework is more important than the quantity. A short, interesting task that encourages students to think, read, or explore a topic may be far more valuable than a long list of repetitive exercises. What is clear is that the purpose of homework should be to support learning, not simply to fill time.

=== medium-long-10 | The Lighthouse Keeper
For more than two hundred years, a lighthouse has stood on a rocky island off the coast. For much of that time, it was looked after by a lighthouse keeper who lived there with his family. Today, the light is automatic, and the island is empty except for the seabirds. But the last keeper still remembers what life was like.

He arrived on the island as a young man with his wife and their newborn daughter. There was no electricity at first, and the lamp at the top of the tower had to be lit every evening and kept burning until dawn. Every few hours during the night, he climbed the narrow stairs to check the light and wind the mechanism that made it turn.

Life on the island was simple but demanding. A supply boat came once every two weeks, bringing food, letters, and fuel. In the winter, storms sometimes prevented the boat from coming for a month or more. The family grew vegetables in a small garden protected by a stone wall, and they caught fish from the rocks.

The keeper's most important job was to warn ships of danger. On foggy nights, when the light could not be seen, he sounded a loud horn every thirty seconds. On one occasion, he saw a fishing boat in trouble during a storm. He sent a radio message to the coast, and a rescue team was able to save the crew.

His daughter grew up on the island and went to school by correspondence, sending her homework to the mainland by boat. She says it was a lonely childhood in some ways, but also a magical one. She knew the name of every bird and could read the weather from the colour of the sky.

When the lighthouse was automated, the family had to leave. The keeper says it was one of the saddest days of his life. He still visits the island once a year, and every time he sees the light shining across the sea, he feels as if a part of him is still there.
